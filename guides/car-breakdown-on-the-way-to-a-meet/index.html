Road safety & smart driving

What to do when someone breaks down on the way to a meet

The disabled car needs a safe location and the right help. It does not need six more cars stopping beside it.

10 min read Published July 28, 2026 By Idle

First priority

Protect people before diagnosing the car.

A breakdown changes the plan immediately. The driver concentrates on controlling the car and getting out of the travel lane when that can be done safely. The group continues to a legal stopping place and waits for a clear update.

01

Control the car and signal the problem

Ease off the accelerator, keep the car predictable, signal, and turn on the hazard lights. If the vehicle can move safely, get as far out of the active lane as conditions and official guidance allow. Do not cross traffic or drive farther when steering, braking, a tire, fire, smoke, or another serious condition makes that unsafe.

Stop on the flattest, most visible location available. Set the parking brake, select Park or an appropriate gear, shut down as the owner’s manual directs, and keep the wheels positioned according to local roadside guidance.

Call emergency services now for danger, injury, fire, smoke, a fuel leak, a blocked travel lane, or an unsafe location.

Tell the dispatcher what happened, which direction you were traveling, the road and nearest exit or mile marker, how many people are present, and whether an electric or hybrid vehicle is involved.

02

Give help an exact location

  • Road name or route number and direction of travel
  • Nearest exit, mile marker, intersection, landmark, or rest area
  • Map pin or coordinates, checked against the visible location
  • Vehicle color, model, and where it is relative to the roadway
  • Immediate hazards such as a narrow shoulder, blind curve, smoke, debris, or people near traffic

Send precise location details to emergency or roadside services first. Share them only with the designated group contact, not a public event chat or social post.

03

Staying in or getting out depends on the location

There is no single safe instruction for every roadside stop. A vehicle fully clear behind a barrier is different from one stranded in a live lane, beside fast traffic, near fire, or on unstable ground.

Follow the dispatcher, police, transportation agency, and owner’s manual. If told to exit, use the side away from traffic when possible and move to the location officials identify. Do not stand in front of, behind, or between stopped vehicles. If remaining inside is the safer official instruction, keep seat belts on.

Children, passengers with limited mobility, animals, severe weather, and the physical road layout can change the safest choice. Tell the dispatcher about them.

04

The group response should stay small

Cars behind see the stop

Continue to the next safe location.

Do not stack cars onto a shoulder, block the disabled driver’s view, or force traffic to pass a larger roadside scene.

Driver sends a location

One contact confirms the plan.

Ask if emergency or roadside help has been called, who is in the car, and what the driver needs. Do not crowd the phone with guesses.

A support car may help

Stop only in a legal place fully clear of traffic.

If no safe place exists, meet the driver after the tow or wait at the next stop. Concern does not make an unsafe shoulder safe.

The meet is waiting

Tell the host the group is delayed.

The event schedule can move on. Nobody should rush the repair, tow, or remaining drive to make an arrival time.

05

Diagnose only after the roadside risk is under control

What happened Do not Safer direction
Flat, damaged tire, or wheel contact Work beside moving traffic or use a jack on unstable ground. Use roadside help unless the car is in a safe work area and the owner procedure can be followed.
Overheating, steam, or coolant loss Open a hot pressurized cooling system. Shut down as directed, stay clear, let it cool, and arrange qualified help.
No start or electrical problem Connect cables without the vehicle-specific procedure or assume every battery is in the usual location. Use the owner’s manual and roadside help. Many vehicles have designated jump points.
Smoke, fire, fuel smell, or major leak Restart the car or return for belongings. Move people away, call emergency services, and follow their instructions.

A safe parking lot can allow a visual check. It does not make an uncertain brake, steering, tire, fuel, cooling, or internal engine problem safe to drive.

06

Electric and hybrid vehicles need vehicle-specific handling

Do not touch exposed high-voltage components or damaged orange cables. The high-voltage battery cannot be jump-started. Some vehicles allow a separate 12-volt battery or designated points to be jumped, but the owner’s manual decides the procedure.

Towing method matters because moving a driven axle can damage an electric or hybrid drivetrain. Tell the roadside provider the exact model, drive configuration, damage, warning messages, and that the vehicle is electric or hybrid. Use the manufacturer’s towing instructions.

Call emergency services for sparks, smoke, flames, crash damage, flooding, or suspected high-voltage battery damage. Keep people away and tell responders the powertrain type.

07

Give the tow operator the details that change the job

  • Exact vehicle, drive type, transmission, and owner-manual towing requirement

  • Low ride height, splitter, side skirt, diffuser, air suspension, or limited steering

  • Locked wheel, damaged tire, brake failure, stuck gear, or fluid leak

  • Electric, hybrid, all-wheel-drive, or other drivetrain considerations

  • Destination approved by the owner and a plan for passengers

Photograph the car and visible damage from a safe position before loading. Remove loose exterior parts only when it can be done away from traffic.

08

Close the group loop after the car is secure

Once help is on scene or the car is loaded, tell the group only what it needs: everyone is safe, the car has assistance, and the remaining drive or event plan. Do not post the failure as entertainment before the owner has had a chance to deal with it.

Before the next trip, record the symptom, warning lights, conditions, diagnosis, repair, and any damaged parts. A breakdown that “went away” still needs an explanation.

Sources and limits

Local roadside instructions take priority

Road design, traffic, weather, vehicle position, and local law change the safest action. Call emergency services for immediate danger and follow the transportation agency or highway patrol for the road you are on.

  • Massachusetts Highway Assistance Program: roadside emergency guidance for hazards, location information, visibility, and occupant decisions on covered highways.
  • NHTSA: Move Over laws for how approaching drivers should respond to stopped vehicles with flashing lights and the need to check state rules.
  • NHTSA: emergency roadside kit and trip preparation for supplies, routes, tires, recalls, and vehicle readiness.
  • NHTSA: electric and hybrid vehicle safety for qualified service, 12-volt jump guidance, towing, high-voltage hazards, and damaged vehicles.
  • NHTSA: electric-powered vehicle emergency response guides for manufacturer-submitted, model-specific responder and towing information.

Reviewed July 28, 2026. This is general roadside safety information, not emergency dispatch, vehicle diagnosis, towing instruction, or legal advice.
